Ability Is Not Access

Foundations of Access Architecture for Preserved Capacity and Unreliable Functional Availability

Abstract

Disability, accommodation, and support decisions often rely on visible evidence of diminished ability. This creates a problem for people whose underlying cognitive, communicative, intellectual, professional, or practical capacities remain intact while access to those capacities becomes unreliable under ordinary conditions.

The paper proposes a foundational distinction: ability describes what a person is capable of doing; access describes whether the conditions currently permit that capability to become usable action.

Ability Is Not Access is a foundational paper within the broader Access Architecture research program.

Contribution boundaries

Established evidence

The paper draws on existing work concerning episodic disability, workplace accommodation, task interruption, fatigue, disclosure, and fluctuating functional conditions.

Field synthesis

Access Architecture organizes these concerns around the distinction between preserved capacity and functional availability under present conditions.

Proposed terminology

Recognition Paradox, access volatility, accommodation drift, access fragility, context collapse, and re-entry failure are proposed terms within the developing framework.

Open hypotheses and recommendations

Candidate design responses are framework recommendations. Their effectiveness requires intervention-specific evidence and should not be assumed from the framework alone.
